## Facts (verified, with the fiction separated)
- **Fiction (episode rule, not real):** all land ice melts in one night. Narration says so in l3 and corrects it in l6. The flooded-rooftop scene in l5 is the scenario's consequence.
- **Real — floating ice (l2):** NASA Sea Level Change Team (E. Huang, 2 May 2023), https://sealevel.nasa.gov/news/261 : floating ice "actually increases sea level slightly when it melts into the salty sea", about 3% of the effect of grounded ice, because meltwater is fresh and less dense than seawater. Narration says "แทบไม่ล้น / แทบไม่เปลี่ยน", never "zero".
- **Real — ~65 m (l4):** Antarctic Ice Sheet 57.9 ± 0.9 m sea-level equivalent (Morlighem et al. 2020, *Nature Geoscience* 13:132–137, DOI 10.1038/s41561-019-0510-8); Greenland 7.42 ± 0.05 m (Morlighem et al. 2017, *Geophysical Research Letters* 44:11051–11061, DOI 10.1002/2017GL074954); glaciers and ice caps 0.32 ± 0.08 m (Farinotti et al. 2019, *Nature Geoscience* 12:168–173, DOI 10.1038/s41561-019-0300-3). Total ≈ 65.6 m. NSIDC Ice Sheet Quick Facts: Antarctica "about 58 meters", Greenland "about 7.4 meters" — https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-sheets/ice-sheet-quick-facts . Narration says "ราวหกสิบห้าเมตร" and the screen says GLOBAL AVERAGE (local rise differs; ocean thermal expansion and land rebound are left out).
- **Real — Bangkok elevation (l5):** "heights ranging from 0.5 to 1.50 m above mean sea level", with land subsidence still up to 20 mm per year — Aobpaet, Cuenca, Hooper & Trisirisatayawong (2013), *International Journal of Remote Sensing* 34(8):2969–2982, DOI 10.1080/01431161.2012.756596. "สูงกว่าตึกยี่สิบชั้น" is our arithmetic (~3 m per storey × 20 ≈ 60 m < 65 m), not a quote. National Geographic's 2013 "If all the ice melted" map is not cited for Bangkok, because its text does not mention Thailand.
- **Real — timescale (l6):** IPCC AR6 WG1 Summary for Policymakers B.5.4: "sea level is committed to rise for centuries to millennia due to continuing deep-ocean warming and ice-sheet melt" — https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg1/chapter/summary-for-policymakers/
- **Optional visual (not narrated):** Doi Inthanon, Thailand's highest point, is 2,565 m above mean sea level (ONEP, onep.go.th) — could appear as a small "ยังพ้นน้ำ" tag in S5's background.
- **Cliffhanger (l7)** is a question for the viewer, not a claim.
