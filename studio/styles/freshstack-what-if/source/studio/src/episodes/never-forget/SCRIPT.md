## Facts (verified, with the fiction separated)
- **Fiction (episode rule, not real):** you remember everything, permanently, with no way to forget. This is stronger than real HSAM, which covers personal life events and dates, not everything (see l5–l6).
- **Real — forgetting curve (l4):** Ebbinghaus (1885), replicated by Murre, J. M. J. & Dros, J. (2015) "Replication and Analysis of Ebbinghaus' Forgetting Curve", *PLOS ONE* 10(7):e0120644, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0120644. Measured as *savings* (time saved when relearning) on nonsense syllables, one subject each. After 1 day savings were 34% (Ebbinghaus) and 32% (replication), so relearning took about two-thirds of the original effort. Narration names the nonsense syllables and the relearning effort; it does not say "we forget X% of everything".
- **Real — HSAM (l5):** first described as a single case by Parker, E. S., Cahill, L. & McGaugh, J. L. (2006) "A case of unusual autobiographical remembering", *Neurocase* 12(1):35–49, DOI 10.1080/13554790500473680. The name HSAM comes from LePort et al. (2012). UC Irvine's lab page says "over 50" people have been confirmed so far — https://cnlm.uci.edu/hsam (page last modified 17 Sep 2026). Narration says "แค่ห้าสิบกว่าคน ที่ยืนยันแล้ว", not "in the whole world". No real person with HSAM is shown or named.
- **Real — most lab tests about the same (l6):** LePort, A. K. R. et al. (2012) "Behavioral and neuroanatomical investigation of Highly Superior Autobiographical Memory (HSAM)", *Neurobiology of Learning and Memory* 98(1):78–92, DOI 10.1016/j.nlm.2012.05.002. 11 HSAM participants recalled public and personal events and their dates far better than controls, but on most standard laboratory memory tests performed comparably to controls. Narration keeps "ทั่วไป / ส่วนใหญ่", not "every test".
- **Real — false memories (l6):** Patihis, L. et al. (2013) "False memories in individuals with highly superior autobiographical memory", *PNAS* 110(52):20947–20952, DOI 10.1073/pnas.1314373110. HSAM participants were as susceptible as controls to false memories on word lists and to claiming to have seen non-existent news footage, and showed more false memory on a photo-slideshow task.
- **Cliffhanger (l7)** is a question. The script does not claim HSAM makes life harder; none of the papers above tests that.
- **Optional background (not on screen):** Richards, B. A. & Frankland, P. W. (2017) "The Persistence and Transience of Memory", *Neuron* 94(6):1071–1084, DOI 10.1016/j.neuron.2017.04.037 — a perspective article proposing that forgetting helps the brain adapt and generalise ("researchers propose", not "proven").
