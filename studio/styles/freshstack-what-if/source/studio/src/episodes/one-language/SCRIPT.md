# 09 — ถ้าทุกคนบนโลกพูดภาษาเดียวกัน (What if everyone spoke one language?)

Style: `freshstack-what-if` (React SVG / Remotion 4.0.530, stick-figure rig in lit rooms, schematic fact card).
Format: vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, the 1920×1080 FreshStack world cropped to a 9:16 window around x=960.
Voice: JK Thai narration (`shared/voice/scripts/narrate.py`). Target ~34–36 s like ep03/ep05.
Captions: `shared/captions/STYLE.md` — Thai with one highlighted phrase + small uppercase English line.
Characters (invented): Pip (hooded stick figure), ลุงร้านราเมน (round-faced stick figure in a headband, invented shop owner).

## Hook (0–2 s)
Frame 0: Pip's alarm clock rings, the speech bubbles of six passers-by outside the window show six different scripts
(drawn as abstract squiggle glyphs, not real text). At ~0.5 s they all flip at once into the same single shape, "ONE LANGUAGE" stamp + shake.
Title "WHAT IF EVERYONE / SPOKE ONE LANGUAGE?" pops in ≤0.3 s while the narrator asks the question.

## Structure (style formula)
Small personal scenes → one fictional rule → turn on real facts → cliffhanger.

## Facts (verified, with the fiction separated)
- **Fiction (episode rule, not real):** everyone wakes up speaking one shared language; old languages fall out of use.
- **Real — Esperanto (l4):** a constructed language by L. L. Zamenhof, meant as an international second language; the first textbook appeared on 26 July 1887 under the pseudonym "Doktoro Esperanto". Britannica, "Esperanto" — https://www.britannica.com/topic/Esperanto ; "L. L. Zamenhof" — https://www.britannica.com/biography/L-L-Zamenhof . On screen: year only, no book title (Britannica's two entries name it differently) and no person shown as a character.
- **Real — more than 7,000 languages (l5):** Glottolog (Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology), "Glottolog information": "Currently 2026-03-02 there are 7674 spoken L1 languages" — https://glottolog.org/glottolog/glottologinformation . Counts differ by catalogue (ANU 2021 release: "the world's 7,000 recognised languages"), so narration says "มากกว่าเจ็ดพัน", which holds under every count. No exact number on screen.
- **Real — nearly half endangered (l5):** Bromham, L. et al. (2022) "Global predictors of language endangerment and the future of linguistic diversity", *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 6:163–173, DOI 10.1038/s41559-021-01604-y; ANU release (16 Dec 2021): "around half were currently endangered" — https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/938003 . UNESCO (7 Jul 2016) gives a floor: "no less than 40% … are in danger of disappearing" — https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/towards-world-atlas-languages
- **Real — Thailand (l6):** Suwilai Premsrirat (2011), *The Journal of the Royal Institute of Thailand* Vol. III, pp. 54–75 (p. 55): "more than 70 living languages, belonging to five language families" (Tai 24, Austroasiatic 23, Sino-Tibetan 18, Austronesian 3, Hmong-Mien 2) — https://www.orst.go.th/FILEROOM/CABROYINWEB/DRAWER004/GENERAL/DATA0000/00000653.FLP/html/62/ . Because the family counts add up to exactly 70, narration says "ราวเจ็ดสิบภาษา".
- **Cliffhanger (l7)** is a question, not a claim that เกรงใจ is untranslatable. Background only: Wyatt & Promkandorn (2012), "A discourse analysis of the Thai experience of 'being krengjai'", *Intercultural Pragmatics* 9(3): 361–383, found 15 different English glosses for the word.
- **Dropped on purpose:** "a language dies every two weeks". It is a forecast (Crystal 2000, from Krauss 1992), not a measured rate; measured estimates are about one every 6–13 weeks (Boissonneault & Hammarström 2026, *Cambridge Prisms: Extinction*). Not used in narration.
- **Ethnologue not used (2026-11-07):** ethnologue.com shows a human-verification check to automated browsers, so its figures (7,170 / ~44% / 75 for Thailand) could not be confirmed. l5 and l6 were reworded to the sources above instead.
