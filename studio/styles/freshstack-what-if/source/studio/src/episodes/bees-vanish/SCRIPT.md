## Hook (0–2 s)
Frame 0: close-up of a flower, a bee lands; at ~0.4 s it blinks out with a small "pop" and a dotted outline where it was,
the flower droops. Wide: Pip in the garden, every bee in the air pops out at once. Title "WHAT IF ALL BEES / VANISHED?"
pops in ≤0.3 s while the narrator asks the question.

## Structure (style formula)
Small personal scenes → one fictional rule → turn on real facts → twist → cliffhanger.

## Facts (verified, with the fiction separated)
- **Fiction (episode rule, not real):** every bee on Earth disappears overnight; all other insects and animals stay. The "next season" lychee scene is the scenario's consequence, drawn from the real study in l5, not a forecast.
- **Real — more than three quarters (l4):** IPBES (2016) Summary for Policymakers, *Pollinators, Pollination and Food Production*, Key Message 2: "More than three quarters of the leading types of global food crops rely to some extent on animal pollination for yield and/or quality." DOI 10.5281/zenodo.2616457. Same finding in Klein et al. (2007) *Proc. R. Soc. B* 274:303–313, DOI 10.1098/rspb.2006.3721 (87 crops depend on animal pollination, 28 do not) — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1702377/
- **Guardrail:** "rely to some extent" ≠ "would vanish". IPBES puts only 5–8% of current global crop production as *directly attributable* to animal pollination, and the 35% figure is the volume of pollinator-dependent crops, not the loss. Neither number is used on screen, and the script never says food would disappear.
- **Guardrail:** pollinators ≠ bees. IPBES Key Message 6 notes more than 20,000 species of bees, plus flies, butterflies, birds, bats and others — which is why the rule keeps the other animals and why durian survives.
- **Real — lychee in Thailand (l5):** Sinhaseni, K. & Catterall, C. P. (2021) "Tropical fruit production depends on wild insect communities: bees and lychees in Thailand", *Journal of Tropical Ecology* 37(1):26–34, DOI 10.1017/S0266467421000067. In northern Thailand, bagging flowers to exclude insects decreased fruit set to one-fifth; bees were about 83% of flower visitors.
- **Real — rice (l6):** cultivated rice is predominantly self-pollinating, with natural cross-pollination typically under 1% (IRRI / CGIAR crop genebank rice profile, https://cropgenebank.sgrp.cgiar.org/images/file/rice/Rice_ENG.pdf); Klein et al. 2007 class cereals as wind- or passively self-pollinated, not pollinator-dependent. Narration says "เป็นส่วนใหญ่", not "100% without bees".
- **Real — durian (l6):** Bumrungsri, S., Sripaoraya, E., Chongsiri, T., Sridith, K. & Racey, P. A. (2009) "The pollination ecology of durian (*Durio zibethinus*, Bombacaceae) in southern Thailand", *Journal of Tropical Ecology* 25(1):85–92, DOI 10.1017/S0266467408005531. Fruit bats, especially the cave nectar bat *Eonycteris spelaea*, are the major pollinators, although the giant honey bee was the most frequent visitor; no mature fruit came from insect pollination. Study: 8 semi-wild trees, 2003 and 2005. Visual shows bees are not needed for durian; narration does not say bees never visit.
- **Cliffhanger (l7)** is a question, not a claim.
